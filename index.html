Number — Revision Notes

Key ideas and methods you need for IGCSE Mathematics.

1. Integers

Integers are whole numbers, including negative numbers, zero and positive numbers.

Examples:

−5, −2, 0, 3, 7

Rules for adding integers

Same signs → add the numbers and keep the sign.

Different signs → subtract the smaller number from the larger and use the sign of the larger number.

−7 + −3 = −10

−8 + 5 = −3

2. Fractions

A fraction represents part of a whole.

In the fraction 3/5, 3 is the numerator and 5 is the denominator.

Adding and subtracting fractions

Fractions must have a common denominator.

1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4

5/6 − 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3

Multiplying fractions

Multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators.

2/3 × 4/5 = 8/15

3. Decimals

Decimals represent fractions using place value.

For example:

0.5 = 1/2
0.25 = 1/4
0.75 = 3/4

Remember the place values:

tenths → hundredths → thousandths

4. Percentages

Percentage means "out of 100".

25% = 25/100 = 1/4

Finding a percentage of a quantity

20% of 80
= 20/100 × 80
= 16

5. Ratio

A ratio compares quantities.

2 : 3

This means that for every 2 parts of one quantity, there are 3 parts of the other.

Simplifying ratios

Divide every part by the same number.

12 : 18
= 2 : 3

6. Indices

An index tells us how many times a number is multiplied by itself.

2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

Important index laws

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ

aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ

(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ

7. Standard Form

Standard form is used to write very large or very small numbers in a convenient way.

a × 10ⁿ

where:

  • 1 ≤ a < 10
  • n is an integer
4,500,000 = 4.5 × 10⁶

0.00072 = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴

8. Bounds and Accuracy

When a measurement has been rounded, the original value lies within a range called its bounds.

For example, if 8 cm is correct to the nearest centimetre:

Lower bound = 7.5 cm
Upper bound = 8.5 cm

The lower bound is included, but the upper bound is not.

Worked Examples

Follow each example step by step. Try to understand the method before looking at the answer.

Example 1: Integer Operations

Question: Calculate:

−8 + 13 − 6

Step 1: Start with −8 and add 13.

−8 + 13 = 5

Step 2: Subtract 6.

5 − 6 = −1

Answer: −1

Example 2: Fractions

Question: Calculate:

2/3 + 1/4

Step 1: Find a common denominator.

The LCM of 3 and 4 is 12.

Step 2: Convert both fractions.

2/3 = 8/12

1/4 = 3/12

Step 3: Add.

8/12 + 3/12 = 11/12

Answer: 11/12

Example 3: Percentage of a Quantity

Question: Find 15% of 240.

Step 1: Write 15% as a fraction.

15% = 15/100

Step 2: Multiply.

15/100 × 240 = 36

Answer: 36

Example 4: Ratio

Question: Divide 84 in the ratio 3 : 4.

Step 1: Add the ratio parts.

3 + 4 = 7 parts

Step 2: Find the value of one part.

84 ÷ 7 = 12

Step 3: Find each share.

3 × 12 = 36

4 × 12 = 48

Answer: 36 and 48

Example 5: Indices

Question: Simplify:

2³ × 2⁴

Step 1: The bases are the same.

Step 2: Add the powers.

2³ × 2⁴ = 2³⁺⁴

Step 3: Simplify.

2⁷ = 128

Answer: 128

Example 6: Standard Form

Question: Write 4,500,000 in standard form.

Step 1: Move the decimal point until there is one non-zero digit before it.

4,500,000 → 4.5

Step 2: Count the places moved.

The decimal point moved 6 places to the left.

Step 3: Write the answer.

4.5 × 10⁶

Answer: 4.5 × 10⁶

Example 7: Bounds

Question: A length is measured as 8 cm correct to the nearest centimetre. Find the lower and upper bounds.

Step 1: Half of 1 cm is 0.5 cm.

Step 2: Subtract 0.5 from 8.

Lower bound = 7.5 cm

Step 3: Add 0.5 to 8.

Upper bound = 8.5 cm

Answer:

7.5 ≤ length < 8.5 cm

Fractions

Master fractions through clear notes, worked examples, practice and exam-style questions.

📘 Revision Notes

1. Types of Fractions

A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than its denominator.

Example: 3/5

An improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator.

Example: 7/4

A mixed number contains a whole number and a fraction.

Example: 1 3/4

2. Equivalent Fractions

Equivalent fractions have the same value.

1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8

3. Simplifying Fractions

Divide the numerator and denominator by their highest common factor.

Example: 12/18 = 2/3

4. Adding and Subtracting Fractions

If the denominators are the same, add or subtract the numerators.

2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7

If the denominators are different, find a common denominator first.

1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6

5. Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

2/3 × 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2

6. Dividing Fractions

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 2/3 × 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6

✏️ Worked Examples

Example 1: Simplify 18/24

Highest common factor of 18 and 24 = 6

18 ÷ 6 = 3
24 ÷ 6 = 4

Answer: 3/4

Example 2: Calculate 2/3 + 1/6

Common denominator = 6

2/3 = 4/6

4/6 + 1/6 = 5/6

Answer: 5/6

Example 3: Calculate 5/6 − 1/4

Common denominator = 12

5/6 = 10/12

1/4 = 3/12

10/12 − 3/12 = 7/12

Answer: 7/12

Example 4: Calculate 3/5 × 10/9

3 × 10 = 30

5 × 9 = 45

30/45 = 2/3

Answer: 2/3

Example 5: Calculate 3/4 ÷ 2/5

Change division to multiplication by the reciprocal.

3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8

Answer: 1 7/8

Integers

Integers are whole numbers that can be positive, negative, or zero.

1. Positive and Negative Integers

Positive integers are numbers greater than zero: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ...

Negative integers are numbers less than zero: −1, −2, −3, −4, −5, ...

Zero is neither positive nor negative.

2. The Number Line

On a number line, numbers increase as you move to the right and decrease as you move to the left.

For example: −5 < −2 < 0 < 3 < 7

3. Adding Integers

When adding integers with the same sign, add their values and keep the sign.

Example: −4 + (−6) = −10

When the signs are different, subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger absolute value and keep the sign of the larger value.

Example: −8 + 13 = 5

4. Subtracting Integers

Remember: subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite.

Example: 7 − 10 = 7 + (−10) = −3

Another example: −5 − (−8) = −5 + 8 = 3

5. Multiplying Integers

Use these sign rules:

Positive × Positive = Positive

Negative × Negative = Positive

Positive × Negative = Negative

Negative × Positive = Negative

Example: −6 × −4 = 24

6. Dividing Integers

Division follows the same sign rules as multiplication.

Example: −24 ÷ 6 = −4

Example: −24 ÷ −6 = 4

7. Order of Operations

When a calculation contains several operations, follow the correct order of operations.

Brackets → Indices → Multiplication/Division → Addition/Subtraction

Example: −3 + 4 × 5 = −3 + 20 = 17

IGCSE Mathematics — Year 10 — Number

What is Standard Form?

Standard form is a way of writing very large or very small numbers using powers of 10.


A number written in standard form has the form:

a × 10n

where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n is an integer.


Example 1 — Large Number

Write 4500000 in standard form.

Move the decimal point until the first number is between 1 and 10:

4.5 × 106

The decimal point moved 6 places to the left, so the power is +6.


Example 2 — Small Number

Write 0.00072 in standard form.

7.2 × 10-4

The decimal point moved 4 places to the right, so the power is −4.


Remember

Large number → positive power of 10

Small number → negative power of 10


Quick Practice

Try these without looking at the examples:

  1. Write 6300000 in standard form.
  2. Write 0.00045 in standard form.
  3. Write 8.2 × 105 as an ordinary number.
  4. Write 3.7 × 10-3 as an ordinary number.

Before moving on, make sure you can convert numbers both ways between ordinary form and standard form.
